The Cutover Decision

passage_id: p_cutover_decision

The control room at Plant Four was dead silent except for the hum of emergency cooling fans. At 02:00 hours, the migration team attempted the final cutover from the twenty-year-old proprietary inventory database to the centralized SAP core. Within forty minutes, real-time telemetry from three automated flour silos flatlined. Batch tracking for the morning shift vanished from the floor dashboards.

Elena Vance, VP of Operations, stood behind the lead systems architect. The external integration consultants insisted the anomaly was merely a transient synchronization lag that would resolve itself before dawn. However, plant manager Mateo Silva pointed to the blinking red status indicators on line three, warning that manual overrides would force an emergency halt of the primary sourdough mixers within the hour.

Vance looked at the board-mandated Go-Live deadline staring back from her tablet. Pausing the rollout meant missing quarterly targets and absorbing massive consulting overruns, but pushing forward risked corrupting master inventory records across all five regional baking plants.
