The Bakery Budget Crisis

passage_id: art_01

At sunrise, the procurement manager stared at the latest supplier quote for flour and edible oils. Global market prices had jumped twenty percent overnight due to poor weather in key farming regions. The local bakery chain operated on tight margins, and every batch of bread or pastry depended on stable ingredient costs. The finance director walked into the office with a printed report, asking if they should lock in a three-month fixed contract immediately or wait to see if the market calmed down. Choosing wrong meant either losing profit on every loaf sold or raising prices so high that regular customers would walk away to competitors down the street.
