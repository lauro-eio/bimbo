The Midnight Handover

passage_id: p001

The control room of the polymer processing plant was dead silent except for the hum of cooling towers. At 3:00 AM, shift supervisor Elena stared at the handover log. The incoming crew had reported two near-miss incidents on the extrusion line within forty-eight hours. Both involved operators working their fourth consecutive night on a rapid backward-rotation schedule.

Elena knew the plant manager was under immense pressure to maintain continuous output, but the fatigue levels among the senior technicians were becoming a safety hazard. When she suggested freezing the current shift rotation to give the exhausted crew an extra recovery day, the plant scheduler pushed back hard. He argued that halting the sequence would trigger costly overtime and disrupt the entire monthly quota. Elena looked at the operator sitting across from her, rubbing his bloodshot eyes, and realized the current system was breaking the people keeping the facility alive.
