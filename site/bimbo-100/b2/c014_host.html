The Bakery Return Dilemma

passage_id: p_art_014

The loading dock at the Central Distribution Hub was piled high with grey plastic crates. Inside sat three hundred loaves of artisan sourdough and country grain bread, all stamped with yesterday's date. Elena, the regional supply chain director, looked at the invoice total and shook her head. Supermarket chain MetroMarket had sent every single loaf back under their full-credit return policy.

For months, MetroMarket store managers insisted that empty shelves hurt weekend foot traffic, so they routinely ordered twenty percent more stock than historical sales figures suggested. The drivers dropped fresh crates every single morning at dawn, quietly hauling away the previous day's unsold stock without a second glance.

Elena knew the current routine was unsustainable. Reducing delivery frequency from daily to three times a week would force managers to forecast carefully, but it risked empty shelves during peak shopping hours. On the other hand, maintaining daily drops meant watching thousands of dollars in fresh product turn into waste every week. She pulled up the regional delivery schedule and prepared for a tense meeting with the retail buyers.
