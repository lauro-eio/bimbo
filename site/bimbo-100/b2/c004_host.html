The Baking Line Incident

passage_id: art_c004

The control room monitor blinked red at 2:14 AM. Conveyor belt section four on the primary baking line was running three degrees hotter than its baseline threshold. Shift supervisor Marcus stared at the thermal graph, debating whether to halt the entire assembly for an unscheduled check or push through until the morning shift. Stopping the line meant losing four thousand units of dough and delaying distribution trucks across three regions. Ignoring the reading risked a catastrophic bearing seizure that could shut the factory down for days. Marcus reached for the radio, called the lead maintenance technician, and asked if the newly delivered vibration sensors could give a definitive reading. The technician sighed, looking at the unconnected sensor boxes sitting on the workbench, waiting for budget approval that had stalled for three weeks.
