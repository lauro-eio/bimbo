The Bakery Margin Crisis

passage_id: art_c001_vignette

The procurement room at Granary Foods smelled faintly of burnt toast and cold coffee. Elena stared at the glowing terminal, watching white wheat futures climb another four percent before noon. Edible oil tariffs had just shifted, and refined sugar contracts were locked in a volatile upward trend. Across the desk, Marco dropped a printed purchase order for three thousand metric tons of flour. We cannot absorb this margin squeeze for another quarter, Marco said, tapping his pen against the laminated forecast. If we do not lock in fixed pricing now, our retail partners will drop our distribution contracts by autumn. Elena scrolled down to view the hedging proposals from the financial desk. One option offered complete protection against price surges but required heavy upfront liquidity. Another option left them exposed to spot market dips while capping losses. The clock on the wall ticked loudly as they weighed the risk of signing tomorrow versus waiting out the market.
