The 4:00 AM Sanitation Dilemma

passage_id: art_c013

The night shift at the automated bakery is winding down, but Line Three is running thirty minutes behind schedule. Elena, the plant operations manager, stands by the cooling conveyors as the digital dashboard flashes a yellow warning for surface protein residue near the mixer hopper. Her lead sanitation technician, Mateo, approaches with a tablet. He points out that the quick rinse will take fifteen minutes, but a full chemical sanitization cycle requires forty-five minutes, which will guarantee missing the morning truck departure for major supermarket clients. The shift supervisor argues that the residue is minor and can be wiped down manually while the line keeps moving. Elena looks at the sanitation logs, then at the clock. If she stops the line for a complete protocol reset, twenty thousand units of dough will sit idle and distribution targets will shatter. If she lets production roll with a quick manual wipe, she risks failing the upcoming third-party audit and breaching core safety thresholds.
