The Procurement Trap

passage_id: p_01

Sofia stared at the latest milling projections on her monitor, watching the curve for edible oils spike upward for the third straight month. As senior procurement manager for a regional bakery distributor, her desk was buried in requests from regional directors demanding relief from surging flour and sugar tariffs. Her CEO poked his head into her office, pointing at an email from their primary wheat supplier. The supplier offered a fixed six-month contract, but at a twenty percent premium over current spot prices. Locking in meant securing continuity, yet risking a massive overpayment if global harvests recovered by autumn. Refusing meant gambling that spot prices would stabilize before their warehouse reserves ran dry next quarter. Sofia cleared her throat, tapped her pen against the desk, and reached for the phone to call the risk committee.
