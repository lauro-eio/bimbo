The Midnight Cutover

passage_id: art_c003_midnight_cutover

At 11:45 PM, the lights in the control room of Plant Three flickered. On the main terminal, the legacy inventory ledger blinked offline for the final time. For eighteen months, corporate headquarters promised that migrating to the new cloud enterprise resource planning platform would streamline scheduling, unify supply chains, and eliminate manual spreadsheet errors across all regional baking plants.

Instead, the midnight go-live hit an immediate wall. The automated mixing line sensors refused to handshake with the new middleware. Raw flour silos showed zero stock in the system while physical bins overflowed on the floor. Shift supervisor Elena checked her phone; three regional plant managers were already messaging her demanding manual override codes.

IT Director Marcus stared at the red error logs cascading down his monitor. He had two choices: halt production entirely across four plants while engineers patched the interface, or revert to manual paper logs for the morning shift and risk violating health traceability compliance. The clock was ticking toward the six o'clock morning dispatch.
